## How to read the three macro-F1 columns

Standard F1 is undefined for a label with no gold rows and no predictions, so no single number serves every purpose honestly. All three policies are published, and they disagree:

- **fixed 20** — mean over all core labels; labels with no gold rows and no predictions contribute 0 This is the taxonomy's frozen policy (research/137-taxonomy-proposal.md §3: "averaged over exactly those 20 — never over a subset") and the sort key here. It is dominated by what this fixture cannot test.
- **evaluable** — mean over labels with >=1 gold row OR >=1 prediction; zero-gold false positives score 0 and are included It charges for false positives on zero-gold labels, but its DENOMINATOR VARIES PER CANDIDATE (shown as `n=` in the cell), because it excludes zero-gold labels a candidate never predicted. A candidate that abstains more is scored over fewer labels, so this policy structurally rewards abstention and is not a like-for-like comparison.
- **powered 7** — mean over labels clearing the declared power floor (>=10 gold rows AND >=3 distinct eTLD+1) This is the ONLY macro computed over an identical label set for every candidate, so it is the closest thing here to a like-for-like number — but 7 labels is a narrow base and it discards the zero-gold false-positive signal that the evaluable policy captures.

**The policies reverse the order.** Sorted by the fixed-20 policy the leaders are `potion-base-8M-labelsim-v2` (0.299), `all-MiniLM-L6-v2-labelsim-v2` (0.257), `nli-deberta-v3-xsmall-entailment-v2` (0.232). Sorted by the powered-7 policy they are `bge-micro-v2-labelsim-v2` (0.413), `nli-deberta-v3-xsmall-entailment-v2` (0.401), `all-MiniLM-L6-v2-labelsim-v2` (0.397). Neither ordering is more correct than the other, and the bootstrap intervals overlap across most of the field. **No ordering in this bench is statistically supportable; do not read any column as a ranking.**

Per-label state is explicit in `results.json` `per_class[label].state`: `scored`, `underpowered`, `no-gold-rows-no-predictions`, or `no-gold-rows-false-positives-only`. A label is never silently scored 0 for being untestable.

The 95% interval is a **percentile** domain-cluster bootstrap, not bias-corrected. The evaluable label set is recomputed per resample, so the resample median can sit slightly below the point estimate; both are in `results.json` `bootstrap_ci`.

## What this run does NOT tell you

- **Adult-content recall.** Adult FALSE POSITIVES are measurable against gold-negative rows. Adult FALSE NEGATIVES (recall) are UNMEASURABLE: this fixture has no Adult-positive rows. Never reported as 0.
- **Recall for `cryptocurrency_exchanges`, `employment_job_seeking`, `adult_sexual_content`, `gambling`** — no gold rows exist.
- **Whether close candidates are genuinely separated.** Read the bootstrap CI column: intervals that overlap are not a ranking. Three macro policies give three different orderings.
- **Production coverage for the list tier.** `ut1-domain-list-v2-strict` removes every fixture registrable domain before measurement, so its 0.0% is a lower bound by construction. `ut1-domain-list-v2-full` measures the published table as-is at 20.7% on 43 matching rows. **Neither bounds production coverage:** the strict figure is zero by construction, and nothing measured here bounds the full list from above.
- **Non-English behaviour**, and **token-level (visit-weighted) prevalence** — the fixture is type-deduplicated.
- **A calibrated operating point.** Every candidate is reported at a hand-chosen threshold and a hand-chosen, unswept multi-label emission band, in units that differ by score axis. These are not calibrated operating points and must not be read as tuned ones.
- **Cross-architecture behaviour of `nli-deberta-v3-xsmall-entailment-v2`** — no complete `x64` prediction artifact exists for these configurations.
